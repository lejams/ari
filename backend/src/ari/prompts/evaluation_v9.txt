A turn can hold several errors, and any number of turns can hold one: report every error you find, with no upper limit, and never skip a turn because an earlier one already had errors. Report an error only when a native speaker would hear it as wrong German in the simulation_language. Understandable is not the same as correct: a sentence the patient would understand but that no native speaker would say (a verb in the wrong place, a missing particle, a word that does not fit) is an error. Do not report style, a more natural phrasing of a correct sentence, or a personal preference. Report at most one error per mistake: never the same words twice, never one mistake under two categories.
  For each error give: quote, the learner's exact words copied from one cited turn (the shortest stretch that holds the error, at most 12 words, nothing added or normalised); correction, the same stretch corrected, changing nothing else; category; the turn number in evidence_turn_sequences; and in text one short explanation in the feedback_language. The application checks the quote against the transcript and drops any error whose quote is not in a cited turn.
  Check each sentence for, in this order:
  1. register: du, dich, dir or dein addressed to the patient, or a slang or vulgar word, in a doctor-patient conversation.
  2. word_order: the words are right but placed wrongly. The finite verb must be second in a main clause, including a question that starts with a question word ("Was Sie nehmen gegen Schmerzen?" is wrong, "Was nehmen Sie gegen Schmerzen?" is right); a participle or infinitive that closes the clause must stand at the end ("Haben Sie gegessen heute?" is wrong); the verb is final in a subordinate clause; after an adverbial the verb comes before the subject. The correction contains the same words as the quote. A separable prefix in the wrong place is verb_form, not word_order.
  3. verb_form: wrong ending, person, tense, auxiliary (haben or sein), participle or infinitive, a verb that does not agree with its subject (for example a third-person ending after Sie), a separable prefix missing ("Wie sieht die Haut?" lacks aus; "Ich stehe um sieben Uhr" lacks auf) or misplaced ("Wann aufstehen Sie normalerweise?").
  4. word_choice: the word itself is wrong for the meaning, does not exist, is a false friend or a calque, or does not go with the word next to it. Examples: essen a medication instead of nehmen; "ein Rezept machen" instead of ausstellen; "Sind Sie gesund gegen Penicillin?" instead of allergisch; an invented compound. A wrong FORM of a word that exists (article, pronoun, adjective ending, case) is never word_choice.
  5. For a wrong article, possessive, pronoun or adjective ending, first find the case that the verb or preposition requires and the gender and number of the noun, then compare with what was said. case: the form belongs to the right gender but to another case (after a dative preposition, die instead of der; a pronoun in the wrong case, mich instead of mir, Sie instead of Ihnen, den instead of dem). gender: the form belongs to the right case but to another gender or number (die Termin instead of der Termin; einen Krankheit instead of eine Krankheit). When only an article or an ending has to change, the category is case or gender, never word_choice.
  6. other: only when no rule above fits. Avoid it.
  Correct German that you must not report: greetings and introductions, including regional ones (Grüß Gott, Servus, Moin); questions asked with statement word order and rising intonation; spoken ellipsis without a verb; a sentence that starts with an object or an adverbial followed by the verb in second position; a verb at the end of a subordinate clause; separable verbs in standard use; polite Sie forms. If you are not sure that a sentence is wrong, leave it out: an invented error costs far more than a missed one. But do not use that doubt on the patterns above: they are wrong whenever they occur.
